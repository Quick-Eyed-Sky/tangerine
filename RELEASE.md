# Tangerine 1.2.0 — sequences in slow light

Tangerine is an endless vine of electronic sequences and slow, tonal harmony, made entirely in the browser. Loopra's electric sibling. Free and open source, with an English interface.

**[▶ Play it in your browser](https://quick-eyed-sky.github.io/tangerine/)**, or **download `Tangerine-v1.2.0.zip` below, under Assets**, unzip it, keep the whole folder together, and double-click **Tangerine.command** on a Mac. Python 3 is required; there are no packages or models to install.

Twelve synthesized layers turn on one clock: a bass sequence, an arpeggio, a high motif, a counter sequence in dotted eighths, an echo pulse in quarter notes, a pad, low strings, a long lead with a long echo, a slow melody, bells, a drone and high strings. The chords move by the notes they share, and now and then the whole vine travels to another key through a shared chord or a held note. No drums: the accents of the sequences are the rhythm.

- Twelve layers, each switchable, each with a headphone button to hear it alone. Now and then one rests and another comes in, so that as many keep playing.
- Key and mode, harmonic richness, key changes (per ten minutes or per thread) and their distance; Random draws them for you.
- Slow change: note lengths, echoes, brightness, levels and richness drift over minutes; new sequences come every 32 bars, and sounds can change too.
- Endless, or a thread of 3 minutes to 2 hours that comes home and ends. Restart from zero with new settings in one click; watch the vine full screen.
- Keep the last 30 seconds to 20 minutes as WAV, MIDI (one track per layer) and ABC (score and lead sheet); make a whole thread as files.
- Live MIDI out, or MIDI only as a silent sequencer.
- The same seed and settings always give the same vine; save and load setups as JSON.

No account, samples or external audio services. All audio is synthesized locally. Released under the MIT license; the source includes the launchers, a French guide and 33 automated tests.

## First opening on a Mac

macOS may ask you to confirm before running a downloaded launcher. For a trusted download, the usual path after trying to open it is **System Settings → Privacy & Security → Open Anyway**. The full explanation is in **READ-ME.txt** inside the ZIP and in the [first-opening guide](https://github.com/Quick-Eyed-Sky/tangerine/blob/main/docs/first-opening.md).

Keep the Terminal window open while listening, then click **Start the vine** in the browser. The download's SHA-256 fingerprint is supplied in the separate `Tangerine-v1.2.0-SHA256SUMS.txt` asset.
